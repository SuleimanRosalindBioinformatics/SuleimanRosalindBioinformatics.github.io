ALGORITHM TAXONOMY

Dynamic Programming Alignment Paradigms

Comprehensive mathematical taxonomy, recurrence relations, and algorithmic trade-offs across all 7 major pairwise sequence alignment variants in computational biology.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Global Alignment (Needleman-Wunsch): Full end-to-end alignment using Bellman's principle of optimality with match/mismatch scoring matrices (BLOSUM62, PAM250).
  • Local Alignment (Smith-Waterman): Domain and motif identification with free restarts, terminating traceback at 0.
  • Fitting Alignment: Short sequence fit entirely within a larger chromosome without terminal overhang penalties on the target.
  • Overlap Alignment: Suffix-to-prefix overlap detection essential for Overlap-Layout-Consensus (OLC) de novo fragment assembly.
  • Semiglobal Alignment: Complete sequence alignment with free terminal overhang gaps at both 5' and 3' flanks.
  • Gotoh Affine Gap Penalties: 3-state DP state-machine (M, Ix, Iy) modeling distinct gap-opening and gap-extension penalties in $O(nm)$ time.
  • Hirschberg Linear Space Alignment: Divide-and-conquer space reduction computing exact global alignments in strictly linear $O(n+m)$ memory via optimal middle-edge detection.

GRAPH THEORY SHIFT

Genome Assembly Paradigms: OLC vs De Bruijn

The computational paradigm shift from Overlap-Layout-Consensus (Hamiltonian path) to De Bruijn graph sequence assembly (Eulerian walk), plus paired-end $(k, d)$-mer gapped reconstruction.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Overlap-Layout-Consensus (OLC): Reads form vertices, overlaps form edges. Assembly maps to finding a Hamiltonian path visiting every vertex exactly once — an NP-complete problem intractable for high-throughput short reads.
  • De Bruijn Graphs (DBG): Reads are shredded into $(k-1)$-mer vertices connected by $k$-mer directed edges. Assembly maps to finding an Eulerian path visiting every edge exactly once — solvable in strictly linear $O(V+E)$ time using Hierholzer's algorithm.
  • Gapped De Bruijn Graphs & Paired Reads: Utilizing $(k, d)$-mer paired-end reads to resolve genomic repetitive elements larger than single read lengths.
  • Contig Generation & Bubble Popping: Non-branching path maximal traversal and heuristic error correction in sequencing read sets.
Representative Rosalind Problems:

COMPUTATIONAL COMPLEXITY

Complexity & Asymptotic Bounds Matrix

Master taxonomy mapping all 284 solved problems to their asymptotic time bounds ($O(n)$ streaming scans, $O(n \log n)$ divide-and-conquer, $O(nm)$ dynamic programming, $O(n^3)$ cubic RNA folding, and NP-complete heuristics).

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Linear Time $O(n)$: Frequency arrays, single-pass nucleic acid counting, GC skew calculation, KMP prefix function matching, and 2-break distance computations.
  • Log-Linear Time $O(n \log n)$: Suffix array construction, divide-and-conquer inversions, sorting, and geometric interval queries.
  • Quadratic Time $O(nm)$: Needleman-Wunsch, Smith-Waterman, Gotoh affine alignment, distance matrix computations, and spectral convolution.
  • Cubic Time $O(n^3)$: Nussinov RNA secondary structure base-pair maximization, Zuker thermodynamic folding, and Floyd-Warshall all-pairs shortest paths.
  • NP-Hard / Exponential Heuristics: Multiple sequence alignment (SP-score), large parsimony (NNI tree rearrangements), and median string search.

STOCHASTIC DECODING

Hidden Markov Models in Computational Biology

Unified mathematical treatment of the Three Classical HMM Problems: Evaluation (Forward-Backward $P(x)$), Decoding (Viterbi $\pi^*$, Soft Decoding), and Parameter Learning (Baum-Welch EM), plus Profile HMMs for protein families.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • The Evaluation Problem: Computing likelihood $P(x)$ of an emitted sequence via the Forward algorithm $\alpha_k(i)$ and Backward algorithm $\beta_k(i)$.
  • The Decoding Problem: Viterbi dynamic programming recurrence $\max_k$ identifying the globally optimal hidden state sequence $\pi^* = \arg\max_\pi P(x, \pi)$ in $O(|Q|^2 \cdot n)$ time.
  • Soft Decoding & Posterior Probabilities: Calculating $P(\pi_i = k \mid x) = \frac{\alpha_k(i) \cdot \beta_k(i)}{P(x)}$ to assess state confidence.
  • Parameter Learning (Baum-Welch EM): Expectation-Maximization iterative updating of transition probabilities $A_{kl}$ and emission probabilities $E_k(b)$.
  • Profile HMMs (Krogh Architecture): Match ($M_j$), Insert ($I_j$), and Delete ($D_j$) states with affine gaps modeling biological protein domain families and pseudocount regularizations.

EVOLUTIONARY INFERENCE

Phylogenetics & Tree Reconstruction Paradigms

Comprehensive mathematical taxonomy of phylogenetic inference: additive trees, distance metrics, 4-point condition, UPGMA, Neighbor-Joining, Small Parsimony (Fitch-Sankoff), and split distances.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Additive Metric Spaces & 4-Point Condition: Additive distance matrix properties where bunched sums satisfy Buneman's 4-point condition for tree compatibility.
  • Distance-Based Reconstruction: UPGMA (ultrametric molecular clock) and Saitou-Nei Neighbor-Joining (NJ) eliminating star trees via rate-corrected distances $D_{ij}^*$.
  • Small Parsimony (Fixed Topology): Fitch's set-intersection algorithm for unit weights and Sankoff's dynamic programming recurrence for arbitrary cost matrices.
  • Large Parsimony (Topology Search): Nearest Neighbor Interchange (NNI) heuristics exploring the superexponential space of unrooted binary trees $(2n - 5)!!$.
  • Phylogenetic Distances & Splits: Robinson-Foulds distance and split distances measuring topological dissimilarity between rival evolutionary hypotheses.

PROTEOMICS & SPECTROMETRY

Peptide Sequencing & Mass Spectrometry Synthesis

Tandem MS/MS formulations ($b$/$y$ ion series), branch-and-bound cyclopeptide sequencing, leaderboard beam search, spectral convolution, DAG spectrum graphs, and PSM dictionaries.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Ion Series Fragmentation: Theoretical masses of $b$-ions (N-terminus) and $y$-ions (C-terminus) under collision-induced dissociation (CID).
  • Branch-and-Bound Cyclopeptide Sequencing: Exact de novo peptide sequencing for idealized, noise-free spectra pruning non-matching prefixes.
  • Leaderboard Beam Search: Robust heuristic sequencing with leaderboard trimming (top $N$ candidates with ties) on experimental spectra with missing/noise peaks.
  • Spectral Convolution: Pairwise peak mass difference multiset identifying constituent amino acids without prior alphabet assumptions.
  • Spectrum Graph DAGs: Longest-path dynamic programming on directed acyclic spectrum graphs to reconstruct optimal amino acid paths in $O(V+E)$.
  • Spectral Dictionaries & PTM Alignment: DP algorithms computing dictionary size $N(m, t)$ and $p$-value statistical significance for Peptide-Spectrum Matches.

FULL-TEXT INDEXING

String Indexing & Pattern Matching Synthesis

The full-text indexing hierarchy: Prefix/Suffix Tries, Suffix Trees (Ukkonen), Suffix Arrays, the Burrows-Wheeler Transform (BWT), and the FM-Index with $O(m)$ exact backward search and approximate matching.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Suffix Trees: Compact tree of all suffixes storing edge intervals $(start, end)$ in $O(n)$ space; solves exact pattern queries in $O(m)$ time.
  • Suffix Arrays (SA): Lexicographically sorted array of suffix starting positions. Binary search matches patterns in $O(m \log n)$ with $5\times$ lower memory than Suffix Trees.
  • Burrows-Wheeler Transform (BWT): Reversible permutation sorting cyclic rotations, concentrating character runs for maximum data compression.
  • Last-to-First (LF) Mapping: Property enabling exact backward string reconstruction and search directly within compressed BWT space.
  • The FM-Index (BetterBWMatching): Combines BWT, LF-mapping, and checkpointed rank vectors to query arbitrary patterns in $O(m)$ time independent of genome length.
  • Approximate Search: Seed-and-extend via the Pigeonhole Principle ($k+1$ partitions) and recursive backtracking on BWT intervals.

STRUCTURAL GENOMICS

Genome Rearrangements & Synteny Synthesis

Signed permutations, Hannenhalli-Pevzner duality theorem, breakpoint graphs, cycle decomposition, and the universal 2-Break Distance Theorem ($d = b - c$) for multi-chromosomal karyotypes.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Synteny Blocks & Signed Permutations: Modeling chromosomal gene orders with signed integers $\pm \pi_i$ representing strand orientations.
  • Breakpoint Analysis: Identifying discontinuities in synteny adjacencies; proving every reversal can reduce at most 2 breakpoints.
  • The Hannenhalli-Pevzner Theorem: Transforming signed permutation sorting into alternating cycle decomposition of the Breakpoint Graph $G(P, Q)$.
  • 2-Break Operations: Universal graph operation replacing two edges with two rewired edges, unifying reversals, translocations, fusions, and fissions.
  • The 2-Break Distance Theorem: Strictly linear $O(n)$ computation of evolutionary distance $d(P, Q) = b - c$ between arbitrary linear or circular multi-chromosomal genomes.

STOCHASTICS & COMBINATORICS

Combinatorics & Population Genetics Synthesis

Mendelian inheritance laws, Wright-Fisher Markovian genetic drift, Catalan/Motzkin non-crossing matchings in RNA secondary structure, and Nussinov/Zuker thermodynamic folding models.

Theoretical Framework & Paradigms:

  • Mendelian Segregation Probabilities: Exact combinatorial distributions of dominant and recessive alleles under binomial transmission models.
  • Wright-Fisher Genetic Drift: Discrete-time absorbing Markov chains modeling allele frequency fluctuations, fixation probability $\pi_i = \frac{i}{2N}$, and geometric decay of heterozygosity.
  • Catalan RNA Matchings: Counting perfect non-crossing base-pair configurations ($C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$) with dynamic programming recursions modulo $10^6$.
  • Motzkin RNA Structures: Enumerating secondary structures with unpaired bases, loops, and bulges.
  • Nussinov Dynamic Programming: $O(n^3)$ polynomial time algorithm maximizing total Watson-Crick and wobble base pairs in RNA strands.
